An interpretive atlas of the supplied notes

01

The premise

Give each map one job.

The notes connect symbolic stories, numerical relations, music and a world called Kingdom. Their diagrams become easier to use when we ask what each one describes: sequence, condition, movement or spatial relation.

In the source

Routes describe before and after.
Realms describe what coexists.

This distinction is explicit. The sphere is more provisional: an attempt to imagine Kingdom as a coherent whole, with music superimposed on the Earth’s surface.

The structural interpretation

Several lenses.
One invented world.

The atlas gives the material a working storytelling vocabulary. Its scene sentence, composition ladder and explanatory matrix are interpretive tools, not a claim to reproduce all of the notes’ numerical logic.

Invention first. System second.

A paraphrase of the opening principle: invent from imagination, then discover or construct a system that supports the idea. Once chosen, its relations can become meaningful creative constraints.
02

The kernel

A scene is a change in conditions.

The recurring terms Miljø, Diktat and Akse can be read as a scene grammar: a situation, a determining pressure and a means of transformation. These are functions; they need not each be a person.

“In condition X, pressure Y acts through Z, leaving a changed world.”

Invented teaching example

One scene, two settings

03

The component maps

Separate the questions.
Keep the relationships.

A change can have a place in a route, a condition in a realm, a direction of transformation and a position in the world. None of those answers is sufficient on its own.

B / Nine Realms

In what condition does it exist?

The realms describe simultaneous structure: internal, intermediate and external relations across hierarchy, duration and experience. A thing’s place in the plot is different from its structural condition.

Simultaneous state-space
The source adds a process protocol. Its example of “5” treats earlier processes as completed when looking from inside out; from outside, the reading reverses. That perspective-sensitive numerical logic is richer than this teaching matrix and remains unformalized here.
C / Axes

What kind of change?

Directional logics of transformation. These describe relations, not necessarily physical travel.

Vertical / hierarchy
Origin, authority, containment, dependency.

Horizontal / duration
Persistence, repetition, development, continuity.

Diagonal / experience
Encounter, perception, mediation, translation.

D / Sphere

Where, relative to the whole?

World topology: centre, layers, surface and crossings. The notes propose this diagram; they do not complete it.

Depth / radial relation
The notes suggest H as outside-in and W as inside-out.

Surface / P
A perceivable or published song; MM is considered as an underlying template.

Crossing / relation between layers
A topology can organize the world without deciding the sequence of its stories.

E / Composition

From vocabulary to world.

The notes make compounds central: meaning depends on relations between components, their fields and their unfoldings. The following ladder is a proposed way to carry those relations into writing.

05

Legend & working guide

Use the map after the spark.

Begin with something worth imagining: a voice, a place, a conflict, a musical encounter. Then use the system to see its relations and decide which ones to develop.

Invent the situation.

Describe what someone hears, wants, does or discovers. Leave the symbol assignment open.

Find the scene sentence.

Name the environment, the determining pressure and the axis of action. State what is different afterward.

Read through the separate lenses.

Route: what follows? Realm: what condition coexists? Axis: what changes? Sphere: how does this relate to the whole?

Test the continuation.

Is there one immediate path or a prism of possibilities? A branch should change a relation, not merely add another label.

Encode only what earns its place.

Use symbols and compounds to record meaningful relations. Return to the source before claiming a numerical identity, equivalence or unfolding.

A reusable scene card

Questions that make it concrete.

Environment
What already exists, and what does it allow?
Dictat
What pressure determines or constrains action?
Agent & object
Who or what acts? On whom or what?
Axis
Does the change concern hierarchy, duration, experience—or a combination?
Realm condition
What is internal, intermediate or external here? From whose perspective?
Topology
What lies within, beneath, beside or beyond this situation?
Transformation & exit state
Which relation has changed? What becomes possible next?
Continuation
One immediate path, several paths, or an unresolved possibility?
Terms / temporal

Route ≠ realm

A route orders events. A realm describes a condition. A figure can enter a plot early while already having complex structural relations.

Terms / functional

Role ≠ identity

Environment, Dictat and Axis name functions in this reading. A place, command, relationship or musical object may carry a function.

Terms / relational

Symbol ≠ fixed meaning

A compound’s fields and relations matter. This atlas does not turn every symbol into a one-word psychological archetype.

06

Intended uses

Write stories.
Compose encounters. Build worlds.

The notes’ musical setting is more than a metaphor. They imagine songs as places, tracks shared and replaced over time, listening as navigation, and participation as something that changes the world.

Ⅰ

Story development

Find the pressure inside a scene and the relation changed by an action. Compare routes without forcing every story into one plot formula.

Useful output: a sequence of scene cards.

Ⅱ

Interactive narrative

Design meaningful prismatic nodes. Preserve the world’s simultaneous conditions while a participant selects one route through them.

Useful output: a branch map with explicit consequences.

Ⅲ

A navigable musical world

Give songs location, layers and duration. Explore loops that admit new material, tracks shared between pieces, and rooms that answer a player’s riff.

Useful output: an encounter score for a place.

A creative framework, not a finished engine. The notes leave major questions open: how the four stories combine, how numerical relations become concrete interactions, and how shared musical space is maintained. The atlas organizes these questions; it does not claim to resolve them.

Let the invention lead.
Let the system make its relations visible.

Read a short passage from the opening principle
“Som jeg har det, så vil jeg være fri til at opfinde frit fra fantasien, uden nogen tilknytning til noget system.. Derefter giver det mening at finde et system - der bekræfter mine egen ide..”

Close English rendering: “As I see it, I want to be free to invent freely from imagination, without any attachment to a system. Afterwards, it makes sense to find a system that confirms my own idea.”

The opening goes on to favour new work and transferred principles over reproducing an existing story. It also allows the structural system to remain in the background, implicit in the actions a scene makes possible.
